# Morphology

Week 3. Composite bodies, and whether the parts form a grammar.

```notes
State the rule on the next slide before naming a single creature. The deck
argues one thing, and the creatures are evidence for it rather than its
subject.
```

---

{/* _class: impact */}

**A composite body is assembled from a small closed set of parts.**

The parts, and not the whole, are what a taxonomy can count.

---

## The ordinary composite

<blockquote lang="zh-Hant">
  <p>有獸焉，其狀如馬而白首，其文如虎而赤尾，其音如謠，其名曰鹿蜀，佩之宜子孫。</p>
  <cite>南山經，南山經之首，杻陽之山</cite>
</blockquote>

<span lang="zh-Hans">鹿蜀</span> (lùshǔ): a horse, a tiger's markings, a red
tail. Two animals and a colour.

---

## A second ordinary case

<blockquote lang="zh-Hant">
  <p>其中多玄龜，其狀如龜而鳥首虺尾，其名曰旋龜，其音如判木，佩之不聾，可以為底。</p>
  <cite>南山經，南山經之首，杻陽之山</cite>
</blockquote>

<span lang="zh-Hans">旋龟</span> (xuánguī): a turtle, a bird's head, a viper's
tail. Three animals, named as three.

---

## Why those are easy

Both halves are animals a reader already classifies. The composite borrows
their diagnoses and adds nothing.

A taxonomy can file it without deciding anything new.

```notes
This is the position the rest of the deck attacks. If every composite were
like these two, morphology would be a solved chapter and week 3 would not
exist.
```

## Count the parts

| Specimen                           | body  | head  | face  | foot / claw | wing | tail       | back        |
| ---------------------------------- | ----- | ----- | ----- | ----------- | ---- | ---------- | ----------- |
| <span lang="zh-Hans">帝江</span>   | sack  | —     | none  | six         | four | —          | —           |
| <span lang="zh-Hans">陆吾</span>   | tiger | —     | human | claws       | —    | nine       | —           |
| <span lang="zh-Hans">天吴</span>   | tiger | eight | human | eight       | —    | eight, ten | blue-yellow |
| <span lang="zh-Hans">开明兽</span> | tiger | nine  | human | —           | —    | —          | —           |

An em dash means the record does not say.
<span lang="zh-Hans">帝江</span>'s face is the record saying there is none.

```notes
Read the table down the columns, not across the rows. Every column is a slot
the four records either fill or leave empty, and no record reaches for a slot
outside the set.
```

---

## The set is small

Body, head, face, claw, foot, wing, tail, back.

Eight part-words, and colour, which is not a part but attaches to one. The four
records use nothing outside that list. The vocabulary is closed before the
counting starts.

## No animal in it

<span lang="zh-Hans">帝江</span> is compared to a sack. Not a horse, not a
tiger, not a bird.

<span lang="zh-Hant">其狀如</span> names an object, and the parts stay
countable: <span lang="zh-Hant">六足四翼</span>.

```notes
The comparison clause is the mechanism the previous two creatures ran on, and
here it points outside the animal kingdom. Nothing in the entry form notices.
```

---

## It still dances

<span lang="zh-Hant">是識歌舞</span> — it knows song and dance.

A body with no face and no animal in it is still described, still filed, still
<span lang="zh-Hant">神</span>. Recognition is not doing the work. Counting is.

---

## Same mountain, same parts

<blockquote lang="zh-Hant">
  <p>神陸吾司之。其神狀虎身而九尾，人面而虎爪；是神也，司天之九部及帝之囿時。</p>
  <cite>西山經，西次三經，昆侖之丘</cite>
</blockquote>

<blockquote lang="zh-Hant">
  <p>開明獸身大類虎而九首，皆人面，東嚮立昆侖上。</p>
  <cite>海內西經</cite>
</blockquote>

## The difference is the office

The nine moves from the tails to the heads, and the rest holds.

<span lang="zh-Hans">陆吾</span> is <span lang="zh-Hant">神</span> and
<span lang="zh-Hant">司天之九部</span>. <span lang="zh-Hans">开明兽</span>
carries <span lang="zh-Hant">獸</span> in its own name. Anatomy does not
separate them. The job does.

```notes
This is the second failure, and it is the opposite of 帝江's. There the parts
worked and the whole was unrecognisable. Here the parts match and the filing
still differs, on a ground morphology cannot see.
```

---

## <span lang="zh-Hans">天吴</span> disagrees with itself

Two records, one name. <span lang="zh-Hant">八足八尾</span> in
<span lang="zh-Hant">海外東經</span>, <span lang="zh-Hant">虎身十尾</span> in
<span lang="zh-Hant">大荒東經</span>.

Only <span lang="zh-Hant">八首人面</span> survives both.

---

{/* _class: impact */}

**The whole is not stable enough to name.**

The parts are. Count those.

```notes
The rule, restated after the evidence. A part can be diagnostic where the whole
is not, which is the only defensible position left after 帝江, 陆吾 and 天吴.
```
