This is the only lecture with a deck. The argument is that composite bodies are
combinatorial, and a combination is easier to see than to read. [Slides](/decks/week-03/)
carry the diagrams; this page carries the claim.

## Outline

- Six terms and two integers: the vocabulary the 山海经 (Shānhǎijīng) composites
  are built from
- Counts as characters, and 陆吾 (lùwú) against 开明兽 (kāimíngshòu) as the
  minimal pair
- A count that moves between records, which is 天吴 (tiānwú)
- Iterated parts in other traditions, briefly, for scale
- 帝江 (dìjiāng), and what a vocabulary does with a body outside it

## Vocabulary plus arithmetic

A composite creature is not a chimera in the loose sense. The text does not
combine arbitrary animals. It draws on a short list of recognisable parts and
varies the number, and the numbers it chooses are almost always odd and small:
one, four, six, eight, nine. Read that way, the bodies form a system, and the
system is countable without any interpretation at all.

The price is that the whole creature stops being the unit. 陆吾 and 开明兽 become
two values of one expression rather than two animals, which is either an insight
or a category error, and the lecture does not settle which.

## Where it stops

A vocabulary is falsified by one word it cannot spell. 帝江 has no face, and
absence has no term. A system whose response to a specimen is to record a blank
is a system that has met something it does not describe, and pretending
otherwise is the habit this course exists to break.

## Preparation

Bring your own six terms, derived from any two composite records. Do not read
帝江 in advance. The session is better if the vocabulary is built before the
creature that breaks it is in the room.
