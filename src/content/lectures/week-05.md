The first of the stress weeks. A character that repeats across unrelated
specimens is not describing the specimens, and this lecture spends its time on
what to do once that is admitted.

## Outline

- The consequence slot as it looked in week 2, and why it was attractive
- 見則天下大旱 as a string, counted rather than interpreted
- Scope against event: a grid with two axes and a small number of cells
- Local omens, which behave differently, and 毕方 (bìfāng) as the example
- Omen as a property of the reader's situation rather than of the animal

## What the omen actually records

The omen states what the sighting means, and a meaning needs somebody for it to
mean something to. Drought matters to a farmer and flood matters to a town. Read
that way the slot is not malformed at all: it is a well-formed record of a
relation between a creature and a population, and we have been trying to file it
as anatomy.

This is the first place where the course's own method takes the damage instead
of the text. The text is consistent. Our column was the wrong shape.

## The move it licenses

Two columns, not one, and neither called *omen*. A scope, which is a population,
and an event, which is a kind of harm. Both are then sortable, and the sort
groups creatures by who is endangered rather than by what the creature is. That
is a real classification of a real thing, and it is not a taxonomy.

Keeping that distinction visible is what the week 7 proposal is marked on.

## Counting, in advance

without the list is not evidence, and the lists will disagree, which is the part
worth two hours.
