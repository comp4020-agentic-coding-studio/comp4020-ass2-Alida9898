A reconstruction is a taxonomic act carried out in a medium that has no way to
mark which parts are evidence. This lecture proposes an audit, and then shows
the one condition the audit needs.

## Outline

- Why a model cannot be silent: size, gait, texture and weight are not optional
- 夫诸 (fūzhū) in three clauses, and the six decisions they leave open
- Omen into agent: what a reconstruction does to 見則其邑大水
- 夔 (kuí), whose single foot has to be animated
- 帝江 (dìjiāng): 渾敦無面目, and drawing a negation
- 九尾狐 (jiǔwěihú), where the copies have replaced the source

## The condition

The audit is simple. Print the record. Print the reconstruction. List every
property the second has and the first does not. Mark each as consistent with
the record, inconsistent with it, or unresolvable from it.

This only works while the record is short enough to keep in mind whole. 夫诸's
record is three clauses, so a list of twelve additions reads against it and a
thirteenth is noticeable. A source of two pages defeats the method. The
山海经's (Shānhǎijīng) brevity, an obstacle in every week since week 2, is for
one week an advantage.

The interesting category is *unresolvable*. Most additions land there, and a
reconstruction that never does has quietly resolved something. 帝江's
facelessness is the limit case: no way of drawing it is either licensed or
forbidden by the record.

## The claim

Reconstruction is not the opposite of scholarship. It is scholarship with the
citations stripped out. The only thing wrong with a game's bestiary is that it
does not say which of its fields came from a line, and week 12's admissibility
rule is the fix.

## Before the session

Bring one existing reconstruction of any creature in the bestiary, with the
record it claims to be of. If you cannot find the record, bring it anyway and
