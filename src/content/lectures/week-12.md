Twelve weeks of rules, and one survives. This lecture states it, shows what it
costs, and then answers the question the course opened with.

## Why declarations and not kinds

The system failed at the level of kinds, in week 9, for reasons that were the
text's and not ours. What did not fail was the practice of writing down which
fields the source filled. That survives because it makes no claim the sources
cannot support: it is a claim about our own document.

So the rebuilt system is thin. No higher ranks, because week 7 showed the
text's own kind-words do not nest. No omens as characters, because week 5
showed they attach to the formula. No trust in a name, because week 8 showed a
name can postdate every record behind it. What remains is a citation
requirement and a declaration requirement.

## The answer

Can a rigorous classification be built for the creatures of the 山海经
(Shānhǎijīng). Partly. What works records what the text says and marks where it
stops. What fails is everything that would make it Linnaean, and it fails for
one reason: there are no specimens, so a name has nothing to anchor to.

That is a result, not a shrug.
