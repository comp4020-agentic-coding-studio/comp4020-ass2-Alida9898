A taxonomy needs characters before it needs kinds. This lecture argues that the
山海经 (Shānhǎijīng) supplies three, that it supplies them in almost every
record, and that the third one is not what it appears to be.

## Outline

- The record as the unit: what is inside one, what is outside it, and why the
  mountain it sits under is not part of it
- The comparison slot, and the fact that it is always to a creature the reader
  is assumed to know
- The counted part, which is the only slot the text states precisely
- The consequence slot, and the first sign that it is not a property of the
  animal
- Optional slots: voice, self-naming, use

## What the three slots are worth

The comparison slot is the weakest of the three and is doing the most work. 其狀
如馬 is only informative to a reader who has seen a horse, so the slot encodes a
reader as well as a creature. Everything a modern taxonomy would call a
character — proportion, dentition, count of individuals — is either absent or
compressed into that one comparison.

The counted part is the strongest slot, because a count is checkable against
another record of the same name. Week 3 does exactly that, and two of the counts
move.

## A warning about the third slot

The consequence slot looks like the most useful, because it is the only slot
tying a creature to an event. Week 5 shows it is the least useful, because the
same sentence attaches to creatures sharing no part. Do not build a system on it
before then, and notice how tempting it is to.
