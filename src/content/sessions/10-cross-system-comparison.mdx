## What this week does

Week 9 set two systems beside ours and stopped there: the
*Physiologus*-descended bestiary, which files a creature by what it signifies,
and the yōkai catalogue, whose unit is the encounter. This week files one
creature under two systems at once and writes down what each filing left out.

Four creatures, four ways two filings fail to agree:

1. **Two drop lists that are complements** —
   <span lang="zh-Hans">夔</span>, whose record splits down the middle, each
   system keeping the half the other discards.
2. **A clause no system here can hold** —
   <span lang="zh-Hans">帝江</span>'s <span lang="zh-Hant">是識歌舞</span>.
3. **The creature only our system fails** —
   <span lang="zh-Hans">驺吾</span>, which both of last week's systems file
   without effort.
4. **A drop list whose entry is a date** —
   <span lang="zh-Hans">九尾狐</span>, where the thing that needs recording is
   not a feature.

There is no fifth section ranking the three systems. Ranking requires a job
named first, and that is done inside the four.

## Example 1: two filings that drop opposite halves

<span lang="zh-Hans">夔</span> (kuí) has one record, in
<span lang="zh-Hant">大荒東經</span>.

<Specimen name="夔" />

File it by week 3's method, parts and integers, and four cells come out of it:
body ox, colour <span lang="zh-Hant">蒼</span>, horn none-stated, foot one.
Everything else in the line is residue — its light is the sun and moon
(<span lang="zh-Hant">其光如日月</span>), its voice is thunder
(<span lang="zh-Hant">其聲如雷</span>), and going in and out of water it always
brings wind and rain (<span lang="zh-Hant">出入水則必風雨</span>). Three clauses, and more of the
record by length than the four cells took.

Now file it by signification. The *Physiologus* asks what an animal means. A
light that is the sun and moon, a voice that is thunder, and weather that
follows the creature in and out of water make a complete entry. The ox body and
the one foot are incidental to it: the shape a sign wears is the least of what
a sign does.

Both filings work. Neither is short of material. Set the two drop lists side by
side and they are complements — ours discards what theirs keeps, and nothing
about <span lang="zh-Hans">夔</span> is lost between the two.

That is not the good news it sounds like. Our card records a grey one-footed ox
and says nothing about weather. Their entry records a weather sign and says
nothing about feet. The two systems contradict each other nowhere, because they
never make a statement about the same clause. What they disagree about is which
half of the line is the creature, and neither file records that there was
another half.

<Caution label="A drop list is not a defect report">
  Every system drops something. A list of what one dropped is a description of
  its scope, not a complaint about its quality, and a system that dropped
  nothing would be the record retyped.

  The list is worth writing because two of them can be compared. One drop list
  on its own says only what you already chose in week 1.
</Caution>

## Example 2: the clause no system here can hold

<span lang="zh-Hans">帝江</span> (dìjiāng) was week 3's best case. Six feet,
four wings: the parts vocabulary survived the body least likely to fit it.

<Specimen name="帝江" />

One clause was residue then and is residue still.
<span lang="zh-Hant">是識歌舞</span> — it knows song and dance. Try it in each
of the three files:

- **Ours.** Not a part, not a count, not a place. Week 3 sent it to residue,
  and residue is a holding area rather than a field.
- **Signification.** The *Physiologus* needs a meaning, and a meaning is
  supplied by a tradition of reading the animal. No tradition supplies one for
  a faceless sack that dances. The entry cannot be written.
- **The encounter catalogue.** It needs a where, a when, and what happens to
  the person. The record gives <span lang="zh-Hant">天山</span> and stops.

Three systems, three unrelated reasons, the same clause on the floor each time.
A feature that no filing can take is the one result this method produces by
itself, and it is what week 11 is handed.

## Example 3: the creature only our system fails

<span lang="zh-Hans">驺吾</span> (zōuwú) stands in
<span lang="zh-Hant">海內北經</span>.

<Specimen name="驺吾" />

Week 9 found that <span lang="zh-Hant">珍獸</span>, treasure-beast, is a price
and not a kind. File the record three ways and the pattern from Example 1 turns
over.

Signification takes it immediately: what a creature is worth to whoever owns it
is a meaning, and it is the most specific thing the line says. The encounter
catalogue takes it immediately too —
<span lang="zh-Hant">林氏國</span> is the where, riding is what happens, and
<span lang="zh-Hant">日行千里</span>, a thousand <span lang="zh-Hant">里</span> in a
day, is the outcome.

Ours produces an accurate card and drops both. Size by comparison, a colour
count and a proportion all file correctly, so the body is not the problem. The
valuation has no field, and the use has none either, because week 5 withdrew
uses as characters of the creature on the grounds that a use states what a
person gets. That ruling was right and this is what it costs.

Two creatures on one page, then, and the systems change sides between them. On
<span lang="zh-Hans">夔</span> the two external systems split. On
<span lang="zh-Hans">驺吾</span> they agree with each other and ours is the
outlier.

<Caution label="Better is not a property of a system">
  Name the job before calling one system better. Ours answers *what kind of
  thing is this*. It is poor at *what happens if I meet it*, *what is this for*
  and *who says so*. Those are the questions week 1 chose not to ask.

  A sentence of the form "system A is better than system B", with no job named
  in it, is not a finding.
</Caution>

## Example 4: a drop list whose entry is a date

<span lang="zh-Hans">九尾狐</span> (jiǔwěihú) files easily everywhere.

<Specimen name="九尾狐" />

A fox shape and nine tails go into our inventory without argument.
Signification does better still: week 9 noted that significance is exactly what
later readers supplied to this creature, so the entry almost writes itself.

Week 7 is the problem. The record names no beast, and
<span lang="zh-Hans">九尾狐</span> is a handle attached afterwards. So what
needs filing here is not a feature at all. It is a date — which parts of the
entry stand in the line and which arrived later. Our card has no field for it,
the *Physiologus* has none, and the catalogue has none. The bestiary marks this
one entry `unnamedInSource`, which is a flag on a single record and not a
column anything else can be read against.

Run this week's method on
<span lang="zh-Hans">九尾狐</span> and it returns nothing. All three systems
keep the same body and drop the same missing date, so there are no opposed drop
lists to compare. The method finds what systems disagree about. It is silent
about what they have in common, including what they have all failed to record.

## What you leave with

Two drop lists for one creature, written before either filing is called better,
and the job each system is being judged at named on the same page as the
verdict.

Carry the clauses no filing could take. <span lang="zh-Hans">帝江</span>'s
<span lang="zh-Hant">是識歌舞</span> is the clearest, and
<span lang="zh-Hans">九尾狐</span>'s missing date is the one that defeats the
method rather than the systems. Week 11 leaves the
<span lang="zh-Hans">山海经</span> (Shānhǎijīng, the *Classic of Mountains and Seas*) for material nobody built these systems for,
and asks which of our characters that material declines to supply.
