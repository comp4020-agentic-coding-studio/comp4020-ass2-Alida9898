## What this week does

Week 6 found that the text's kind-words track a job rather than a body. This
week goes after the field above them, the one nothing has questioned since week
2: the name. A name is the handle a record is filed under, and a handle can
outlive the record and keep collecting.

Four sections:

1. **The record with no name in it** —
   <span lang="zh-Hans">九尾狐</span>, whose line names a mountain and no beast.
2. **What the name collected afterwards**, and why the record is too short to
   stop it.
3. **The same process inside the edition** —
   <span lang="zh-Hans">狌狌</span> under two graphs and three bodies, and
   <span lang="zh-Hans">讙头</span> drifting the other way.
4. **The counter-case** — <span lang="zh-Hans">精卫</span>, where what the name
   grew was already in the line.

## The record that names no beast

<Specimen name="九尾狐" />

Count the names in that line. There is one, and it is
<span lang="zh-Hant">青丘之山</span>, the mountain. The beast has none. It is a
beast shaped like a fox, with nine tails, an infant's voice, an appetite for
people, and a use.

<span lang="zh-Hans">九尾狐</span> (jiǔwěihú) is not in the edition. The
bestiary prints the name because a reader needs a handle and the entry declares
that it is doing so, with <code>unnamedInSource</code> set. This is the single
most consequential fact in the course and it costs one line of reading to
confirm, which is why the first spec point asks you to confirm it rather than
believe it.

Note what the last clause does.
<span lang="zh-Hant">食者不蠱</span> — eat it and you cannot be bewitched. The
creature is the cure for bewitchment. It is not the cause of it.

## What the name collected afterwards

The fox that takes human form, the fox that seduces, the fox whose tail count
is a count of centuries: none of that is in
<span lang="zh-Hant">南山經</span>. None of it contradicts
<span lang="zh-Hant">南山經</span> either, and that is the mechanism. Three
clauses are too short to be inconsistent with anything. An absence does not
push back.

So the drift is not a sequence of errors. Each addition was made to a name that
had room for it, and the room was there because the record stopped early.

  So treat the list as a description of what the name carries today, which you
  can check against your own prior belief, and not as evidence about any
  particular later text. The evidence in this session is the three records
  below, all of which are in the edition.
</Caution>

## The same drift inside the edition

<span lang="zh-Hans">狌狌</span> (xīngxīng) makes the point without leaving the
book.

<Specimen name="狌狌" />

Three records. In <span lang="zh-Hant">南山經之首</span> it is
<span lang="zh-Hant">禺</span>-like with white ears, going on all fours and
running like a person. In <span lang="zh-Hant">海內南經</span> it knows people's
names and is shaped like a pig with a human face. In
<span lang="zh-Hant">海內經</span> it is a green beast with a human face and the
name is written <span lang="zh-Hant">猩猩</span>. One name, two graphs, three
bodies, and the surviving modern word means an ape in a zoo.

<Specimen name="讙头" />

<span lang="zh-Hans">讙头</span> (huāntóu) drifts in the opposite direction. In
<span lang="zh-Hant">海外南經</span> the entry is a country,
<span lang="zh-Hant">讙頭國</span>, whose people have human faces, wings and
bird bills. In <span lang="zh-Hant">大荒南經</span> the country is gone, the
spelling is <span lang="zh-Hant">驩頭</span>, and what remains is a creature
that eats fish from the sea and walks propped on its wings. A polity became an
animal, or an animal was mistaken for a polity, and the entry form cannot record
which.

## The counter-case

<span lang="zh-Hans">精卫</span> (jīngwèi) is why this week is not a complaint.

<Specimen name="精卫" />

Its record already contains the story. The drowned daughter of
<span lang="zh-Hant">炎帝</span>, the stones carried from the western mountains
to the eastern sea: that is in the line, not attached to it later. What drifted
was which part later readers carried. The bird became a figure for persistence,
and the diagnosis — crow-shaped, patterned head, white bill, red feet — stopped
being the part anyone repeated.

Drift is therefore not the same as invention. Sometimes a name grows what the
record never held, and sometimes it grows one clause of what the record did
hold, at the expense of the rest. A system that records only the surviving
version cannot tell those two cases apart.

## What you leave with

A specimen card carrying a field the earlier cards did not: the name's source.
Where the edition names the creature, cite the line. Where it does not, say who
did, and if the answer is *this course*, say that.

Before the [system proposal](/assessments/system-proposal/) closes at the end of
this week, go through it for every species name the edition does not print, and
mark them. A proposal whose species are named by later readers has imported
later readers' conclusions and filed them as data. Week 8 is where the name
field fails outright.
