The habitat field on every card so far has accepted a string, and a string will
accept anything. This week's rule is that two incompatible kinds of fact have
been going into it, and that the field has been hiding the difference rather
than recording it.

## A route is an itinerary

The 山經 chapters give, for each mountain, a direction and a distance from the
one before. A chain of bearings is a thing you can draw, and the drawing has a
length. 夫诸 (fūzhū) is filed under 敖岸之山, the first mountain of the 萯山 route
in 中次三經. That is a named locus, on a named route, at a known position in a
sequence. Three facts, all of them the same kind of fact, all checkable against
the neighbouring entries.

Whether the drawing corresponds to terrain is a separate question and not this
week's. The point is that a drawing exists to be wrong.

## Where the line stops

陵鱼 (língyú) is the counter-case, and its whole record is short enough to hold
in view:

> 陵魚人面，手足，魚身，在海中。

Four statements about a body and one about the world. 在海中: in the sea. Not
which sea, not measured from where. The field can be filled with those three
characters and nothing further can be derived from them.

The chapter is contested as well. The same line stands in 海內北經 only inside
袁珂's note arguing that nine passages were misfiled, so for this record neither
the habitat nor the 卷 can be stated without an argument attached.

## A habitat that is a polity

讙头 (huāntóu) is worse in a more interesting way:

> 讙頭國在其南，其為人人面有翼，鳥喙，方捕魚。

The locus is a country, and the country is placed relative to the previous
entry: 在其南, to its south. The origin of the coordinate system is the sequence
of entries itself, which means the position is real but only inside the text. In
大荒南經 the same creature appears spelled 驩頭, with no country at all. 驺吾
(zōuwú) is filed at 林氏國 in the inner north, which is likewise a state rather
than a place.

So one field is being asked to hold: a mountain on a measured route, an
unspecified sea, and a country located with respect to whatever was described
previously. Those are three kinds of claim. Calling all three *habitat* is what
the rule objects to.

## In the room

- **Thirty minutes.** 中次三經 drawn on paper from the stated bearings, in
  groups, one mountain per person.
- **Fifty minutes.** The same treatment attempted on 海外南經. Stop at the first
  sentence that cannot be drawn and write the sentence down verbatim. The
  collected sentences are the week's actual result.
- **Forty minutes.** The schema question, argued: one habitat field or two.

## Out of the room

You leave with a position on splitting the field, and a reason for it that is
not tidiness. Whoever writes the system proposal in week 7 either splits the
field or defends keeping it whole, and this session is where the evidence for
both options was collected.
