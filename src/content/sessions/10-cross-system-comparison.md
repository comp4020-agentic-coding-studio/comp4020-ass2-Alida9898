## Why this week exists

Last week you looked at three systems that are not ours: the yōkai catalogue,
the medieval bestiary, the modern database. Looking is easy. The useful move is
to run the same creature through two of them and watch what falls out.

Systems do not disagree about creatures. They disagree about which questions
are worth asking, and you only see that when the same animal has to go through
both.

## Four creatures, two filings each

Take them one at a time. For each, file it under our morphological system from
week 3, then under one other, and write down what the second filing could not
carry.

- **九尾狐** (jiǔwěihú) is the test case, because it has the most baggage. Our
  system can hold the body. The name it is filed under is not in the record at
  all, which is week 7's finding, and no field anywhere holds "acquired later".
- **帝江** (dìjiāng) has six legs, four wings and no face. A system built on
  head characters has nowhere to start.
- **夔** (kuí) is one-footed and brings weather. Our system records the foot and
  drops the weather. A system organised by what a creature does to the world
  records the weather and drops the foot.
- **驺吾** (zōuwú) is the opposite problem: it is valued, and valuation is not a
  character in our system at all.

## What the comparison is actually measuring

A system is good at a job. Name the job before you call a system better.

Ours is built to answer: what kind of thing is this. It is poor at: what will
happen if I meet it, what is this for, who said so. Those are not failures. They
are the questions we chose not to ask in week 1, and the cost of that choice
comes due here.

## In the room

Two hours.

- **Forty minutes.** The four creatures above, filed live, two systems each.
- **Fifty minutes.** Groups. Each group takes one system from last week and
  writes its top-level division, then files three of our creatures in it.
- **Thirty minutes.** Pool the dropped features. The list of things no system in
  the room could hold is what week 11 starts from.
