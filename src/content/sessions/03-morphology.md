Last week's middle slot took any counted part. This week asks what the whole
inventory of parts is, and whether it is small enough to be a vocabulary. The
rule says it is, and that the consequence is a taxonomy of parts rather than of
animals.

## The parts list

陆吾 (lùwú) is a tiger's body, nine tails, a human face, tiger's claws. 开明兽
(kāimíngshòu) is a body mostly a tiger's, nine heads, every one of them
human-faced:

> 開明獸身大類虎而九首，皆人面，東嚮立昆侖上。

Both stand on 昆侖. Their inventories draw on the same six terms — tiger's body,
human face, claws, heads, tails, feet — and differ in which noun the nine
attaches to. Nothing else separates the bodies. The count is the character; the
part is only the vocabulary it is stated in.

天吴 (tiānwú) makes the same point with one creature instead of two. In 海外東經
it has eight human faces, eight feet, eight tails and a blue-yellow back. In
大荒東經 it has eight faces, a tiger's body and ten tails. Eight heads is the
only value surviving both records. If the count is the character, then the two
records describe two species, and a taxonomy has to either choose a record or
admit that its strongest character is unstable.

## The end of the parts list

帝江 (dìjiāng) is where the grammar runs out:

> 有神焉，其狀如黃囊，赤如丹火，六足四翼，渾敦無面目，是識歌舞，實為帝江也。

Six feet and four wings are countable, and they exhaust what the inventory can
take. The comparison slot holds a sack rather than an animal. The distinguishing
feature is the absence of a feature, which no column accepts as a value. The
colour is given by reference to a fire. And the record closes by reporting that
it knows how to dance, which is neither a part nor a count nor a use.

Every other composite here is assembled from animals a reader has met. 帝江 is
assembled from none of them, and its record is no shorter and no vaguer for it.
That is the week's finding, and it is a finding about our method rather than
about the creature.

## Slides

The argument is about shapes, and prose cannot hold four bodies side by side.
[Slides](/decks/week-03/) carry the four inventories drawn as part diagrams,
with the counts as labels. They are the only deck this semester, for that
reason.

## The two hours

- **Thirty minutes.** The six terms, derived in the room from 陆吾 and 开明兽.
- **Fifty minutes.** In groups, extend the vocabulary across ten composite
  bodies and stop at the first part needing a seventh term. Report the part.
- **Forty minutes.** 帝江, collectively, with the inventory on the board and the
  record read one clause at a time. The session ends when the room agrees on
  what to write in the row and not before.

## What the card asks for

This week's specimen card is parts-only: terms and integers, no prose. Anything
that will not decompose goes under a heading reading *residue*, and the residue
is the part of the card that gets read first.
