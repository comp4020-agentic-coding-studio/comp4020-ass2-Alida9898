Linnaeus had specimens. Where he did not have specimens he had drawings, and
where he had neither he had the written testimony of people who claimed to have
stood next to the animal. The system assumes that behind every name there is a
body somebody could in principle produce.

The 山海经 (Shānhǎijīng) offers no bodies. It offers a formula and a location. A
creature is introduced as being like something the reader is presumed to know
already, distinguished from that by a part or two, named, and assigned a
consequence. There is no author to ask, no specimen to measure, and no second
observer.

Twelve weeks go on asking whether a system built for the first situation can be
applied to the second. The answer arrived at is not yes and not no, and the
failures along the way carry more of the argument than the successes.

## The order that is already there

The text is commonly treated as raw material waiting for a system. This week's
rule denies that. The 山海经 arrives sorted, by a principle it applies without
exception, and that principle is worth stating before anything better is
proposed.

The order is geographic. Every creature is filed under the mountain or the water
it is found at, and the mountains are strung along routes running in compass
directions at stated distances. Nothing is grouped by body. The arrangement
answers one question completely: what will I meet if I go there.

That is a classification. It is not the absence of one, and reading it as an
absence is the first mistake this material makes available.

## Four records on one route

狌狌 (xīngxīng), 鹿蜀 (lùshǔ), 猼訑 (bóshī) and 九尾狐 (jiǔwěihú) are all filed
in 南山經之首, the first route of the first chapter, under 招搖之山, 杻陽之山,
基山 and 青丘之山 in that order. They arrive in that order because that is the
order they are walked past.

Two of them have nine tails. 猼訑 has them on a sheep:

> 有獸焉，其狀如羊，九尾四耳，其目在背，其名曰猼訑，佩之不畏。

The beast at 青丘之山 has them on a fox:

> 有獸焉，其狀如狐而九尾，其音如嬰兒，能食人；食者不蠱。

The edition never calls that second one 九尾狐. It is an unnamed beast, and the
name it is filed under here arrived later; week 8 is about how.

The two records sit several mountains apart, and the text nowhere remarks that
they share a count. A system sorting by body would have set them side by side.
This one sets 猼訑 beside whatever else stands on 基山. The disagreement between
those two filings is what the whole course runs on.

## In the session

Two hours, in three parts.

- **Forty minutes** on what a classification is for, with Linnaeus at one pole
  and Borges' invented encyclopedia of overlapping animal categories at the
  other. That list is usually cited to show all classification is arbitrary. The
  opposite lesson is available: the joke works only because a reader can tell
  those categories fail and can say why. A classification that can be wrong is a
  thing that can be done well.
- **Forty minutes** on the 招搖之山 passage, read slowly. We mark up the formula
  and name its slots. Those names are provisional and we keep them all semester,
  so they are worth arguing about now rather than in week 9.
- **Forty minutes** in groups, sorting ten creatures from 南山經 with no rules
  supplied. The point is to notice which rules you reach for unprompted.

## The card that does not count

You leave with the slot names the room agreed on, which week 2 formalises, and
with a specimen card for 狌狌 that you do not hand in. The first card that
counts is due at the end of week 2.
