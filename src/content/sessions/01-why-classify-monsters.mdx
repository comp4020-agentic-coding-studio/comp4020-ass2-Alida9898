This morning you got three moves: choose characters, nest them, name in two
parts. This afternoon you meet the material, and the first thing to establish is
that somebody got here before you.

The 山海经 (Shānhǎijīng, the *Classic of Mountains and Seas*) is usually
described as a chaotic catalogue of monsters. It is not chaotic. It is sorted,
by a principle it applies without a single exception, and you cannot improve on
a system until you can say what the existing one does.

## Start with the cover

山 is mountain. 海 is sea. 經 is classic. The book divides exactly where its name
divides: five chapters of mountains, thirteen of seas. The top level of the
classification is printed on the title page.

The received text has eighteen 卷 (juàn, scrolls). Almost every chapter title is
a compass direction: 南 south, 西 west, 北 north, 東 east, 中 centre.

- Five 山經 (shānjīng, mountain classics) — south, west, north, east, centre.
  Between them, twenty-six numbered routes.
- Four 海外經 (hǎiwàijīng), beyond the seas — south, west, north, east.
- Four 海內經 (hǎinèijīng), within them — south, west, north, east.
- Four 大荒經 (dàhuāngjīng), the great wilderness — east, south, west, north.
  Same principle, different rotation.
- One more, headed 海內經 again, with no direction at all.

Inside a route, each creature is filed under the mountain or the water where it
is found. Not under anything about its body. Nothing in the book puts two
creatures together because they look alike.

So what question is this system answering? What will I meet if I go there. And
it answers that completely.

By this morning's test, that is a classification. It has a character — location.
It applies it to every entry. It never switches to a different kind of question
halfway, which is more than the Celestial Emporium managed.

## Four creatures, one route

狌狌 (xīngxīng), 鹿蜀 (lùshǔ), 猼訑 (bóshī) and 九尾狐 (jiǔwěihú) are all filed
in 南山經之首 (nánshānjīng zhī shǒu), the head of the southern mountain classic,
which is the first route in the book. They stand on four mountains along it, in
this order: 招搖之山 (zhāoyáo), 杻陽之山 (niǔyáng), 基山 (jī) and 青丘之山
(qīngqiū). The suffix 之山 means "mountain of".

They appear in that order because that is the order you would walk past them.
Nothing else determines it.

Now look at what that costs. Two of these have nine tails. 猼訑 has them on a
sheep, at 基山:

> 有獸焉，其狀如羊，九尾四耳，其目在背，其名曰猼訑，佩之不畏。
>
> *There is a beast there, shaped like a sheep, with nine tails and four ears,
> and its eyes on its back. It is called 猼訑. Wear it and you will not be
> afraid.*

The beast at 青丘之山 has them on a fox:

> 有獸焉，其狀如狐而九尾，其音如嬰兒，能食人；食者不蠱。
>
> *There is a beast there, shaped like a fox but with nine tails, and its cry
> is like an infant's. It can eat people. Eat it and you will not be harmed by
> 蠱 (gǔ), poisoning by witchcraft.*

The italic line under each quotation is this course's own translation, printed
inside the quotation so it cannot be mistaken for the record. The quoted lines
stay in the traditional characters the edition prints, because changing the
script of a quotation is still changing the quotation.

Two records, several mountains apart, sharing a feature the text never mentions
they share. A system sorting by body would have filed them together. This one
files 猼訑 next to whatever else stands on 基山.

That disagreement is the course. Everything from week 3 onward is an attempt to
re-file this material by body, and every week it costs something.

One more thing about the second record: the edition never calls it 九尾狐. It is
an unnamed beast. The name it is filed under here arrived centuries later, and
week 7 is about how.

## In the room

Two hours.

- **Thirty minutes.** The table of contents, on the screen, in the edition's own
  order. We read the eighteen headings and mark which are directions.
- **Fifty minutes.** The 招搖之山 passage, slowly. We mark up the formula, and
  name its slots. Those names are provisional and we keep them all semester, so
  argue about them now rather than in week 8.
- **Forty minutes.** Groups. Ten creatures from 南山經, no rules supplied, sort
  them. Then each group says which character it reached for and why. Nobody
  reaches for location, and that is the finding.

## What you leave with

The slot names the room agreed on, which week 2 formalises, and a specimen card
for 狌狌 that you do not hand in. The first card that counts is due at the end of
week 2.
