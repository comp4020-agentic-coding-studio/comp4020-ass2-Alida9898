The consequence slot has looked like the most promising character since week 2.
It is the only one tying a creature to an event in the world, and an event is
the kind of thing a classification can be tested against. This week withdraws
it. The rule's claim is that the omen belongs to the formula and not to the
creature, and the evidence is that identical sentences sit on bodies with
nothing in common.

## The same sentence on a bird and on a snake

顒 (yú) is filed under 令丘之山 in 南次三經:

> 有鳥焉，其狀如梟，人面四目而有耳，其名曰顒，其鳴自號也，見則天下大旱。

The third record under the name 肥遗 (féiyí) is filed under 太華之山 in 西山經之首,
and spelled 肥𧔥:

> 有蛇焉，名曰肥𧔥，六足四翼，見則天下大旱。

Five characters, verbatim, on both. One is an owl-faced bird with four eyes and
ears. The other is a six-legged four-winged snake. No part, no count and no
chapter is shared. If 見則天下大旱 is a character of the bird then it is equally a
character of the snake, and week 3's parts grammar would have to call them
congeneric. Neither conclusion survives being said out loud.

## The grid

The slot is not free text. Across this week's five records it takes two values
on each of two axes. Scope runs 邑, 國, 天下 — the town, the state, the world.
Event runs 旱 and 水, drought and flood. 軨軨 (línglíng) carries 見則天下大水,
which is 顒's sentence with one character exchanged. 夫诸 (fūzhū) carries 見則其邑
大水: the same event at the smallest scope. The snake under 肥遗 carries 見則其國
大旱.

A small closed table of values is exactly what a taxonomic character should look
like. This one qualifies on form and fails on substance, because the cell a
creature lands in is not predictable from anything else on its card.

## The counter-case

毕方 (bìfāng) does not fit the grid. Its consequence is 見則其邑有譌火 — an
uncanny fire, local, and the only fire among these five. Popular tradition
credits 毕方 with drought; the drought belongs to other creatures entirely, and
where the misattribution came from is week 8's material.

If fire is unique to 毕方, then this is the one omen here that does diagnose a
single creature. One usable value in a column of repeats is not a character.
It is a coincidence that has not been noticed yet.

## Counting the rule

The rule says at least six creatures. That is a claim about the edition and not
a fact anyone should accept from a syllabus. The session's first task is
checking it, by reading the five 山經 chapters for the string and not by reading
a translation or an index.

## Shape of the session

- **Twenty minutes.** The grid drawn on the board, empty.
- **Sixty minutes.** Filled from the edition, in pairs, a chapter each. A cell
  with two unrelated creatures in it is the result we want, and the pair that
  finds one reads both records aloud.
- **Forty minutes.** Whether the omen column stays on the card. Somebody has to
  defend keeping it, and the defence is assigned rather than volunteered.

## After

Cards from this week carry the omen split into scope and event as separate
values, so the grid can be sorted by machine before the week 7 proposal. A
student who drops the column instead writes one sentence saying why, on the
card, where the column used to be.
