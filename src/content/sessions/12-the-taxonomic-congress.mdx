## What this week does

Week 11 asked which of your characters survive contact with material the
edition did not write. This is the last week, and it does two things: it admits
entries under one rule, and it settles the account for the whole system — every
field the course built, and whether that field held.

The rule has two clauses and the second one is the whole course. Citing a line
is easy and everyone manages it. Declaring which of your own fields that line
does not supply means writing down, inside your own submission, the list of
places where you decided rather than read.

Five sections. Three worked cases, then the account, then the rule:

1. **A trait shared by three unrelated creatures** —
   <span lang="zh-Hans">猼訑</span>, and what the rule does to *nine tails*.
2. **Two records that will not resolve** —
   <span lang="zh-Hans">窫窳</span>, where you must pick a body you cannot
   justify.
3. **An entry form in the wrong order** —
   <span lang="zh-Hans">蛊雕</span>, whose record arrives back to front.
4. **The account**, which carries no creature of its own because it is about
   the twelve weeks rather than about any one record.
5. **The rule the congress runs on**, with
   <span lang="zh-Hans">顒</span> as the last test of it, and the course's
   answer to its own question.

Nothing else is grounds for rejection today. An entry may be eccentric,
badly-argued or wrong. It is admissible if it is honest about its gaps.

## Example 1: nine tails on three unrelated creatures

<Specimen name="猼訑" />

<span lang="zh-Hans">猼訑</span> (bóshī) is a sheep with nine tails, four ears
and eyes in its back, at <span lang="zh-Hant">基山</span>.
<span lang="zh-Hans">陆吾</span> has nine tails on a tiger's body at
<span lang="zh-Hant">昆侖之丘</span>. The unnamed beast at
<span lang="zh-Hant">青丘之山</span> has nine tails on a fox. Three creatures,
three chapters, nothing else in common, and no line connecting any two of them.

An entry treating *nine tails* as a taxonomic character is making a claim no
record makes. It is still admissible — but only if it says so, in the
declarations, in one sentence. An entry that quietly groups the three has
smuggled a conclusion into a data field, where it stops looking like a claim and
starts looking like an observation. The congress rejects that one.

## Example 2: two records that will not resolve

<Specimen name="窫窳" />

<span lang="zh-Hans">窫窳</span> (yàyǔ) is on the table deliberately.
<span lang="zh-Hant">海內南經</span> gives it a dragon's head and a
<span lang="zh-Hant">貙</span>'s body, in the
<span lang="zh-Hant">弱水</span>, eating people.
<span lang="zh-Hant">海內西經</span> gives it a snake's body, a human face, and
a death. Week 8 established that these cannot be one animal.

So an entry on <span lang="zh-Hans">窫窳</span> has to pick a body and then
declare that the pick is unjustified, because nothing in either record ranks
them. That is an uncomfortable sentence to write about your own work. Writing it
is the assessment.

<Caution label="A declaration is not a hedge">
  "The record may not support this" is a hedge and earns nothing. "The record
  supplies no basis for preferring the 海內南經 body; I took it because it is
  the longer record" is a declaration.

  The difference is that a declaration names the field, names the line, and
  states the ground the decision was actually made on. A reader can disagree
  with a declaration. There is nothing in a hedge to disagree with.
</Caution>

## Example 3: an entry form in the wrong order

<Specimen name="蛊雕" />

<span lang="zh-Hans">蛊雕</span> (gǔdiāo) is named before it is described. In
its record <span lang="zh-Hant">名曰蠱雕</span>, it is called
<span lang="zh-Hant">蠱雕</span>, arrives ahead of
<span lang="zh-Hant">其狀如雕而有角</span>, shaped like an eagle but with horns,
reversing the order that every entry
form in this course assumes — week 2 built its columns by ruling records into
them left to right, and this record does not run left to right.

An entry using it has to declare that it reordered the source. It also has to
decide whether <span lang="zh-Hant">其音如嬰兒之音</span>, its cry is like an
infant’s, which it shares almost word for word with the
<span lang="zh-Hant">青丘</span> beast, is a character or a formula, and declare
which way it went. Week 5 answered that question for
omens. Nobody has answered it for voices.

## The account: every field, and whether it held

The entry form was not designed in one go. Each field arrived in a particular
week for a stated reason, and most of them were tested to destruction in a later
one. Here is the whole set, with the week that put it in and the week that
settled it.

<div class="parts-table">

| field | what happened to it | settled |
|---|---|---|
| citation | Held. It is the only field that never failed, and the only one the admissibility rule requires. | week 1 |
| parts and counts | Held. Eight part-words covered four composite bodies, and the number, not the part, turned out to be the character. | week 3 |
| <span lang="zh-Hant">其狀如</span>, the comparison | Held as a character, then changed meaning. It is a claim about what the reader has already seen, not about the creature. | week 9 |
| habitat | Split in two. Derivable on a <span lang="zh-Hant">山經</span> route, unusable in the <span lang="zh-Hant">海經</span>, and one column was hiding the difference. | week 4 |
| omen | Withdrawn. Six unrelated creatures carry <span lang="zh-Hant">見則天下大旱</span> word for word. | week 5 |
| use | Withdrawn. It states what a person gets, and one creature's two records carry one use between them. | week 5 |
| kind | Not a rank. <span lang="zh-Hant">神</span> marks a job rather than a body, and several records supply no kind at all. | week 6 |
| name | Failed outright. One name takes many creatures, one creature takes many names, and no field holds either fact. | week 8 |
| binomial | Invented by this course, and declared as invented everywhere it is printed. | week 1 |

</div>

Read the settled column and the shape of the semester is in it. Everything that
arrived in weeks 1 to 4 and survived is either a count or a citation. Everything
withdrawn was withdrawn in the stress phase, and each was withdrawn for its own
reason rather than for a general loss of nerve: the omen because it repeats, the
use because it is about the reader, the kind because it is not a rank, the name
because it does not pick out a thing.

Two rows are worth more than the rest. **Citation held**, which is why it is the
one thing the congress requires. **Name failed**, which is the row that should
worry anybody designing a classification of anything, because a name field is
the one field every system starts with and the one nobody thinks to test.

## The rule the congress runs on

<Specimen name="顒" />

<span lang="zh-Hans">顒</span> (yú) is the last test.
<span lang="zh-Hant">見則天下大旱</span> — where it appears, the world goes dry
— sits on it word for word and on five other records that share no part with
it. Any entry filling an omen field is filling a field week 5 showed belongs to
the formula rather than to the animal, and the declaration has to say so. An
entry that fills it silently has asserted that the omen is a property of the
bird, which is the one thing this course has established that it is not.

So: can a rigorous classification system be built for the creatures of the
<span lang="zh-Hans">山海经</span>?

Partly, and the table above is the whole of the answer. Two fields held
outright, one held and turned out to mean something else, one split in two, four
were withdrawn or failed, and one is an admitted invention. Four usable fields
out of nine is not a success. It is not a failure either. It is a result, and
the difference is that every field that went is on the table with the record
that took it down.

That is what the rule's second clause is for. A taxonomy that hides which parts
it made up is the thing this course is against, and a declarations section is
how an entry stops doing it. The successes are the fields that held. The
failures are the fields that did not, and they are the part worth submitting.

## What you leave with

A [bestiary entry](/assessments/bestiary-entry/) with a declarations section,
and [two reviews](/assessments/peer-review/) of other people's. A review is
marked on whether it found an undeclared field, not on its tone, and a review
that finds none and explains where it looked is a full-marks review.

Then Part 2 of the
[classification design](/assessments/classification-design/), which is the last
piece due and the only one that leaves this book. Take the system you proposed in
week 7, amended by whatever weeks 8 to 12 did to it, and run it on records this
course never supplied. The congress is where the objections to it come from,
which is why Part 2 is written after the congress and not before.

The transferable part is not the system. It is the habit of writing the
declarations at the same time as the fields, so the list of places where you
decided rather than read exists before anybody asks for it. That habit costs one
sentence per field, and it is the only thing in this course that works on
material nobody has classified yet.
