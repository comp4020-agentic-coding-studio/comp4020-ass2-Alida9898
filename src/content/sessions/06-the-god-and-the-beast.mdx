## What this week does

Week 5 withdrew the omen and the use from the character set. This week the
system gains a column instead of losing one, and it does not have to invent it:
<span lang="zh-Hant">神</span>, <span lang="zh-Hant">獸</span>,
<span lang="zh-Hant">鳥</span> and <span lang="zh-Hant">人</span> are printed in
the records themselves. Borrowing them is the cheapest move available and the
least examined, because a borrowed rank carries whatever the lender was sorting
by.

Three sections, testing the assumption that what it was sorting by is the body:

1. **Two guards on one mountain, filed in different kingdoms** —
   <span lang="zh-Hans">陆吾</span> and
   <span lang="zh-Hans">开明兽</span>.
2. **What the three <span lang="zh-Hant">神</span> have in common, which is
   nothing** — <span lang="zh-Hans">烛阴</span> and
   <span lang="zh-Hans">帝江</span>.
3. **The records that name no kind at all** —
   <span lang="zh-Hans">精卫</span>, whose record names one and then adds
   something the entry form has no slot for.

## One mountain, two files

<span lang="zh-Hant">昆侖</span> has two guards. Week 3 took both bodies apart
and found the whole anatomical difference was one number moving from
<span lang="zh-Hant">尾</span> to <span lang="zh-Hant">首</span>.

<Specimen name="陆吾" />

<Specimen name="开明兽" />

Both are nine-fold. Both are tiger below and human above. A taxonomy sorting by
body puts them in adjacent cells, and week 3's did.

The text puts them in different kingdoms. Read the first line for its verb:
<span lang="zh-Hant">司之</span>, it administers, and what follows is a
jurisdiction — the nine regions of heaven and the seasons of the god's park. The
body is the middle clause. <span lang="zh-Hans">开明兽</span> is given a
direction to face and a mountain to stand on, and no jurisdiction at all.

So the kind-word is tracking the job. That is a coherent thing to sort by. It is
not what our parts inventory sorts by, and the two produce different files from
the same two records.

## What the three 神 have in common

<span lang="zh-Hans">烛阴</span> (zhúyīn) is the same shape of claim made
larger.

<Specimen name="烛阴" />

Its record supplies exactly one fact about the body, which is that it is a
thousand <span lang="zh-Hant">里</span> long. Everything else specifies what the
creature's states do to the world: eyes open are day, eyes closed are night,
breath out is winter and in is summer. It does not drink, eat or rest, and its
resting would be wind. The most precisely specified creature in the chapter, and
almost none of the specification is about it.

<Specimen name="帝江" />

<span lang="zh-Hans">帝江</span> (dìjiāng) is filed as a
<span lang="zh-Hant">神</span> too and holds no office whatsoever. It is a sack
the colour of cinnabar fire, six feet, four wings, no face, and the only thing
the record says it does is know song and dance.

Three <span lang="zh-Hant">神</span>, three unrelated grounds for the word.
<span lang="zh-Hans">陆吾</span> has a jurisdiction,
<span lang="zh-Hans">烛阴</span> has a consequence, and
<span lang="zh-Hans">帝江</span> has neither. The word is not a rank. It is the
set of records the text declined to call animals.

<Caution label="Not a rank is not the same as not useful">
  <span lang="zh-Hant">神</span> is doing real work in the text. It marks a
  record whose point is function rather than encounter, and that is a
  distinction worth having.

  What it will not do is sit above <span lang="zh-Hant">獸</span> in a hierarchy
  and inherit characters downward, which is what a rank is for. Take the word
  and you have taken a label. Take it as a rank and you have claimed something
  the text never claims.
</Caution>

## The records that name no kind

<span lang="zh-Hans">精卫</span> (jīngwèi) is given as a
<span lang="zh-Hant">鳥</span> and diagnosed properly — crow-shaped, patterned
head, white bill, red feet, calling its own name.

<Specimen name="精卫" />

Then the same record continues into the daughter of
<span lang="zh-Hant">炎帝</span> who drowned in the eastern sea and became the
bird, and who carries wood and stones from the western mountains to fill it in.
One record holding a diagnosis and a biography. The entry form has a slot for
one of those.

Other records name nothing at all. Neither of the two passages behind
<span lang="zh-Hans">窫窳</span> (yàyǔ) says what kind of thing it is, and the
bestiary's <code>kind</code> field is optional for that reason rather than by
oversight. Every proposal has a kind field, so every proposal has to say what
that field does when the source is silent: leave it empty, infer it from the
body, or refuse the entry until somebody argues for one. Those three answers are
three different systems.

## What you leave with

A ruling you have defended out loud, and a note of the objection you could not
answer. One side of the room argues that
<span lang="zh-Hans">开明兽</span> is a misfiled
<span lang="zh-Hant">神</span> and the other that
<span lang="zh-Hans">陆吾</span> is a <span lang="zh-Hant">獸</span> with a job.
Later commentary is not admissible in that argument. Lines are.

The [system proposal](/assessments/system-proposal/) is due at the end of week 7
and is marked on whether its rules are stated tightly enough to be broken, which
is what weeks 8 to 12 do to them. The question it is read for first is what your
kind field does with <span lang="zh-Hans">开明兽</span>.
