## Why this week exists

Last week you got Linnaeus' three moves. The first one is **choose characters**,
and it is the one you cannot start without.

So where do characters come from? You do not invent them. You read the material
and see what it already tells you about every case. Linnaeus could count
stamens because every flower has stamens to count.

The 山海经 hands us a candidate on a plate. Its records are written to a
repeating shape:

> 其状如 X，Y 首，见则 Z
>
> shaped like X, with Y for a head, and when it appears, Z

Three slots. A comparison, a distinguishing part, a consequence. Today we treat
those as our first three characters, rule them into columns, and see how far
that gets us.

It gets us further than you would expect, and then it stops. Finding where it
stops is the actual work of the session.

## Rule the columns

Take 鹿蜀 (lùshǔ), from the first route:

> 有獸焉，其狀如馬而白首，其文如虎而赤尾，其音如謠，其名曰鹿蜀，佩之宜子孫。
>
> *There is a beast there, shaped like a horse with a white head, marked like a
> tiger, with a red tail, and its cry is like singing. It is called 鹿蜀. Wear
> it and your descendants will prosper.*

Ruled into columns:

| slot | value |
|---|---|
| comparison | 馬, a horse |
| distinguishing part | 白首, white head. Also tiger markings, red tail |
| name | 鹿蜀 |
| consequence | 佩之宜子孫, worn, it benefits descendants |

That is a specimen record in four fields, and you could build a key out of a
hundred of these.

Now 蛊雕 (gǔdiāo):

> 水有獸焉，名曰蠱雕，其狀如雕而有角，其音如嬰兒之音，是食人。
>
> *In the water there is a beast called 蠱雕, shaped like an eagle but with
> horns, its cry like an infant's. It eats people.*

Same three slots, but the name arrives **first** and the consequence is not an
omen at all. It is a behaviour: it eats people.

<Caution label="The third slot is not one thing">
  The consequence slot takes at least three unrelated kinds of content. An omen
  (見則天下大旱, a drought follows), a use (佩之宜子孫, wear it for this
  benefit), and a plain behaviour (是食人, it eats people).

  Ruling them into one column makes them look comparable. They are not. Weeks 5
  and 6 are spent taking this column apart, so write down now which kind each
  of your records has.
</Caution>

## Count before you trust it

Last week's lesson was that a classification can be wrong and you can say why.
Apply that to our own rule before we build on it.

This course cites **45 base-text lines**. Of those:

| slot | records carrying it |
|---|---|
| 其狀如, the comparison | 21 of 45 |
| 名曰, the name | 26 of 45 |
| 見則, the omen | 11 of 45 |
| comparison **and** name together | 13 of 45 |

So the formula is not a law the text obeys. It is a template the text reaches
for, and completes fewer than half the time in the records we have chosen.

<Caution label="What that number is and is not">
  Those 45 lines were selected for this course's 25 creatures, not sampled from
  the whole book. They tell you the formula is incomplete in our own data. They
  do **not** tell you the proportion across all eighteen 卷, and no page here
  claims they do.
</Caution>

## An empty slot is a character

Here is the part that matters for everything after week 4.

夫诸 (fūzhū) has a comparison, a name and an omen, and **no use**:

> 有獸焉，其狀如白鹿而四角，名曰夫諸，見則其邑大水。
>
> *There is a beast there, shaped like a white deer with four horns. It is
> called 夫諸. Where it appears, that town floods.*

軨軨 (línglíng) has all three and nothing else:

> 有獸焉，其狀如牛而虎文，其音如欽。其名曰軨軨，其鳴自訆，見則天下大水。
>
> *There is a beast there, shaped like an ox with tiger markings, its cry like
> a groan. It is called 軨軨, it calls its own name, and where it appears, there
> are floods.*

Two floods, two different scopes: 其邑 is one town, 天下 is everywhere. Same
slot, different magnitude, and nothing in the formula records the difference.

And 夫诸's missing use is not "no benefit". It is **no information**. The text
does not say the creature is useless; it says nothing. A taxonomy that writes
`use: none` has invented a fact.

<Caution label="Blank is not zero">
  Your specimen card leaves an unsupplied field visibly blank. Not "n/a", not
  "none", not a dash that could be read either way. If the record is silent,
  the card is silent, and it says which line it is silent about.

  This is the one thing the first assessment fails you for outright.
</Caution>

## In the room

Two hours.

- **Twenty minutes.** The four records above, on screen, ruled into columns
  together.
- **Sixty minutes.** Groups of three. Each group takes five records from
  南山經, rules them, and reports two numbers: how many were complete, and how
  many had a consequence that was not an omen.
- **Forty minutes.** We pool the counts and compare them with the 45-line table
  above. Then we argue about the column names, because we keep them all
  semester.

## Hand in

Your first specimen card, for any creature in 南山經 other than 鹿蜀. Due
Sunday. It cites its line, fills the slots the line supplies, and leaves the
rest blank.

Marked on one thing: whether the blanks are honest.
