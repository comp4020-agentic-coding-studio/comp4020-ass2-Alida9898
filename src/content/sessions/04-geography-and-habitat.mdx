## What this week does

Week 3 left you a vocabulary of parts you can count. The habitat column has been
on the card since week 2 and has never been examined, and it looks as solid as
the parts do. It is not. One field name is holding three kinds of claim that
cannot be compared with each other.

Three records, each filling that field a different way:

1. **A position you can derive** — <span lang="zh-Hans">夫诸</span>, filed at a
   named locus on a named route.
2. **A field that is filled and locates nothing** —
   <span lang="zh-Hans">陵鱼</span>, whose habitat is three characters long.
3. **A position that exists only inside the text** —
   <span lang="zh-Hans">讙头</span>, placed with respect to whatever was
   described before it.

A fourth section puts <span lang="zh-Hans">驺吾</span> beside those three and
asks what one column is supposed to do with all of them.

## Example 1: a position you can derive

The <span lang="zh-Hant">山經</span> chapters give, for each mountain, a
direction and a distance from the one before. A chain of bearings is a thing you
can draw, and the drawing has a length.

<Specimen name="夫诸" />

<span lang="zh-Hans">夫诸</span> (fūzhū) is filed under
<span lang="zh-Hant">敖岸之山</span> on the <span lang="zh-Hant">萯山</span>
route of <span lang="zh-Hant">中次三經</span>. That is a named locus, on a named
route, at a known place in a sequence. Three facts of the same kind, each
checkable against the entries either side of it.

<Caution label="A drawing that is wrong is still the right kind of thing">
  Whether the route corresponds to terrain anybody has walked is a separate
  question and not this week's. The property that matters is that a drawing
  exists to be wrong about. The next two records do not have that property, and
  they do not have it in two different ways.
</Caution>

## Example 2: a field that is filled and locates nothing

<span lang="zh-Hans">陵鱼</span> (língyú) has a record short enough to hold in
view whole.

<Specimen name="陵鱼" />

Four statements about a body and one about the world.
<span lang="zh-Hant">在海中</span>: in the sea. Not which sea, not measured from
anywhere. The cell is full. Nothing can be derived from what is in it, and a
reader who wants to know where to stand has been given the same answer as a
reader who wants to know nothing.

The chapter is contested as well. The line stands in
<span lang="zh-Hant">海內北經</span> only inside
<span lang="zh-Hans">袁珂</span>'s note arguing that nine passages were misfiled,
so for this record neither the habitat nor the
<span lang="zh-Hant">卷</span> can be stated without an argument attached to it.

## Example 3: a position that exists only inside the text

<span lang="zh-Hans">讙头</span> (huāntóu) fails a third way, and this one is
worth more than the other two.

<Specimen name="讙头" />

The locus is a country, and the country is placed relative to the previous
entry: <span lang="zh-Hant">在其南</span>, to its south. The origin of the
coordinate system is the sequence of entries. That makes the position real —
you can say exactly where <span lang="zh-Hant">讙頭國</span> is, as long as you
are reading — and unusable the moment the entry is lifted out of its chapter,
which is what filing it on a card does.

In <span lang="zh-Hant">大荒南經</span> the same creature appears spelled
<span lang="zh-Hant">驩頭</span> with no country at all. One of the two records
supplies a habitat that depends on its neighbours, and the other supplies none.

## What the habitat field is holding

<span lang="zh-Hans">驺吾</span> (zōuwú) lands in the same column.

<Specimen name="驺吾" />

<span lang="zh-Hant">林氏國</span> is a polity, like
<span lang="zh-Hant">讙頭國</span>, and the record places it nowhere further.
So four records put four things in one column: a locus on a measured route, an
unbounded sea, a country fixed by its neighbour, and a country fixed by nothing.

Sorting the cards by habitat now produces a list that looks ordered and answers
no question. That is the cost of a field that accepts a string, because a string
accepts anything. The field is not wrong. It is under-specified, and it hides
the difference instead of recording it.

## What you leave with

A position on splitting the habitat column, and a reason for it that is not
tidiness. Whoever writes the week 7 proposal either splits the field or defends
keeping it whole, and this is the session where the evidence for both options
was collected.

If you split it, say what the second field is called and what goes in it when a
record gives a country. If you keep it whole, say what your sort order means.
Week 5 takes the other column that has been on the card unexamined since week 2,
the one holding what happens when you see the creature.
