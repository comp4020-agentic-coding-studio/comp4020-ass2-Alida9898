## What this week does

Week 10 produced a list of features no system in the room could hold. This week
points the system at material the edition did not produce — a model, a
rendering, a sketch — which is the first test of whether it transfers anywhere.

The test runs in both directions. Some of our characters have nothing to read
off an image: no image supplies a locus, an omen, or a citation. And the image
supplies characters we never had a column for, because a model has a size, a
skin, a gait and a weight whether the record gives them or not.

Three steps and a failure case:

1. **Six decisions from three clauses** —
   <span lang="zh-Hans">夫诸</span>, whose complete record settles none of them.
2. **Where the comparison character runs out** —
   <span lang="zh-Hans">夔</span>.
3. **Drawing an absence** — <span lang="zh-Hans">帝江</span>.
4. **When the audit stops working** —
   <span lang="zh-Hans">九尾狐</span>, where there is no record at the bottom of
   the stack.

The method depends on one accident: these records are short. A reconstruction
from a nineteenth-century field description cannot be audited, because the
description already specifies more than a viewer can hold. Three clauses can be
held in view, and against three clauses every addition is visible.

## Step 1: six decisions from three clauses

<Specimen name="夫诸" />

A white deer, four horns, and a town flooded. That is the complete record. Now
list what a model needs and the record does not supply: how large, where the
four horns sit and whether they branch, what colour its eyes are, how it holds
its head, whether it walks or stands, and whether the water is caused by it or
merely announced by it. Six decisions before anybody has drawn a hoof.

The last one is not a visual question and it changes the creature most.
<span lang="zh-Hant">見則其邑大水</span> — where it appears, that town floods — is a
conditional. A reconstruction
showing the beast summoning a flood has converted an omen into an agent, which
is the move week 5 refused when it kept omens out of the character set. A
renderer makes that decision by drawing, often without noticing there was one.

<Caution label="An addition is not an error">
  Every item on that list has to be decided by anybody who renders anything.
  The audit does not ask a designer to stop deciding. It asks which decisions
  were made by the record and which by the designer, and it can only ask that
  because the record is short enough to check against.

  Mark each addition consistent, inconsistent, or unresolvable. Only the middle
  one is a mistake.
</Caution>

## Step 2: where the comparison character runs out

<Specimen name="夔" />

<span lang="zh-Hans">夔</span> (kuí) has one foot, and the record does not say
how it moves. A model has to move.
<span lang="zh-Hant">出入水則必風雨</span> says it goes in and out of water;
whether that is a wade, a hop, a dive or a stride is supplied entirely by
whoever animates it.

This is the comparison slot failing in a new way. Week 2 took
<span lang="zh-Hant">其狀如</span> as a character because it tells you what to
picture. Here it tells you what to picture standing still, and says nothing
about the thing a moving image cannot avoid. Our best-transferring character
transfers about half way.

## Step 3: drawing an absence

<Specimen name="帝江" />

<span lang="zh-Hans">帝江</span> (dìjiāng) is where this week ends, for the
reason week 3 first noticed. Every other composite in the bestiary is assembled
from animals a reader already knows — a tiger's body, a human face, a snake's
tail. This one is assembled from none of them.

<span lang="zh-Hant">渾敦無面目</span>: a blur, no face. A renderer has to draw
that, and every way of drawing an absence is a positive choice — a smooth
surface, a shadow, a smear, a mask. Each reads differently and none of them is
what the record says, because the record says only that something is not there.
Then, because the line ends
<span lang="zh-Hant">是識歌舞</span>, the thing has to dance.

That clause is the one week 10 found no system could file. Here it becomes
compulsory: an animator cannot leave it in residue.

## When the audit stops working

<Specimen name="九尾狐" />

<span lang="zh-Hans">九尾狐</span> (jiǔwěihú) is the case where the method
fails, and the failure is the useful part. Its reconstructions are not being
made from the record. They are made from earlier reconstructions, which were
made from the tradition week 7 traced, which grew on a line that does not even
name the creature.

The additions are no longer legible, because legibility was never a property of
the image. It was a property of the gap between an image and a short record, and
nobody in that chain is looking at the record. A system whose audit depends on a
source can say nothing at all about material that has lost contact with one.

## What you leave with

An annotated list of your own additions to one creature, in any medium including
a sketch on paper. The list is the deliverable and the picture is evidence for
it, which is the reverse of how reconstructions are normally judged.

That list is also this week's [specimen card](/assessments/specimen-cards/), and
it is the last one before the congress.

This week is the rehearsal for Part 2 of the
[classification design](/assessments/classification-design/), which is the last
thing due. The question is the same one at a larger scale: which of your
characters survive contact with material the edition did not write, and which
turn out to be readable only inside it. Here the new material is a picture of a
creature you already have a record for. In Part 2 there is no record, because
you choose the material yourself.
