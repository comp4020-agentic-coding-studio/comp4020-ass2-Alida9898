## What this week does

Week 4 found one column holding three kinds of claim. This week takes the column
next to it — what happens when you see the creature, and what you get if you use
it — and withdraws both from the character set.

Four sections, and the first three are about the omen:

1. **The same five characters on six unrelated bodies** —
   <span lang="zh-Hans">顒</span> and the
   <span lang="zh-Hans">肥遗</span> snake, which share the sentence and nothing
   else.
2. **The grid the omens do fill** — scope against event, with
   <span lang="zh-Hans">軨軨</span> one character away from
   <span lang="zh-Hans">顒</span>.
3. **The omen no other record carries** — <span lang="zh-Hans">毕方</span>.

The fourth section is about the use instead, and it is the one that catches
<span lang="zh-Hans">旋龟</span> and <span lang="zh-Hans">猼訑</span>.

## Example 1: the same five characters on six unrelated bodies

<span lang="zh-Hans">顒</span> (yú) is filed under
<span lang="zh-Hant">令丘之山</span> in <span lang="zh-Hant">南次三經</span>.

<Specimen name="顒" />

The third record under the name <span lang="zh-Hans">肥遗</span> (féiyí) sits in
<span lang="zh-Hant">西山經之首</span>, at
<span lang="zh-Hant">太華之山</span>, and spells the name
<span lang="zh-Hant">肥𧔥</span>.

<Specimen name="肥遗" />

<span lang="zh-Hant">見則天下大旱</span>, five characters, verbatim on both. One
is an owl-shaped bird with a human face, four eyes and ears. The other is a
six-legged, four-winged snake. No part, no count and no chapter is shared. If
those five characters are a character of the bird then they are equally a
character of the snake, and week 3's parts grammar would have to call the two
congeneric.

Four more records carry the same sentence, and the session's first task is
finding them in the edition rather than accepting them here:
<span lang="zh-Hant">獙獙</span> at
<span lang="zh-Hant">姑逢之山</span> and <span lang="zh-Hant">薄魚</span> at
<span lang="zh-Hant">女烝之山</span>, both in
<span lang="zh-Hant">東山經</span>;
<span lang="zh-Hant">䱻魚</span> at
<span lang="zh-Hant">子桐之山</span>, also eastern; and
<span lang="zh-Hant">鱄魚</span> at <span lang="zh-Hant">雞山</span> in
<span lang="zh-Hant">南次三經</span>. A winged fox, two fish and a third fish,
plus the bird and the snake above. Six records, four
<span lang="zh-Hant">卷</span> sections, one sentence.

## Example 2: the grid the omens do fill

The slot is not free text, and that is what makes it tempting. Across these
records it takes a small number of values on each of two axes. Scope runs
<span lang="zh-Hant">邑</span>, <span lang="zh-Hant">國</span>,
<span lang="zh-Hant">天下</span> — the town, the state, the world. Event runs
<span lang="zh-Hant">旱</span> and <span lang="zh-Hant">水</span>, drought and
flood.

<Specimen name="軨軨" />

<span lang="zh-Hans">軨軨</span> (línglíng) carries
<span lang="zh-Hant">見則天下大水</span>, which is
<span lang="zh-Hans">顒</span>'s sentence with one character exchanged.
<span lang="zh-Hans">夫诸</span> carries
<span lang="zh-Hant">見則其邑大水</span>, the same event at the smallest scope.
The <span lang="zh-Hant">北山經</span> snake under
<span lang="zh-Hans">肥遗</span> carries
<span lang="zh-Hant">見則其國大旱</span>, which is the third scope.

A small closed set of values, combining on two axes, is exactly what a
taxonomic character should look like. This one qualifies on form and fails on
substance. Nothing else on a creature's card predicts which cell it lands in,
and nothing in the cell predicts anything else about the creature.

<Caution label="The formula is real; the character is not">
  Do not read this as the text being sloppy. The grid is evidence that the
  text's omen slot is disciplined — three scopes, two events, and the wording
  stable enough that one exchanged character is the whole difference between
  two records.

  What fails is our attempt to file the creature by it. A well-formed field in
  somebody else's system is not a character in yours.
</Caution>

## Example 3: the omen no other record carries

<span lang="zh-Hans">毕方</span> (bìfāng) does not fit the grid at all.

<Specimen name="毕方" />

Its consequence is <span lang="zh-Hant">見則其邑有譌火</span> — an uncanny fire,
at the smallest scope, and the only fire in the set. Popular tradition credits
<span lang="zh-Hans">毕方</span> with drought instead. The drought belongs to
the six records above, and how the misattribution happened is week 7's material.

So this is the one omen here that picks out a single creature. It is still not a
character. A value that appears once is a value nobody has yet found a second
instance of, and treating it as diagnostic is a bet on the six-record pattern
not extending.

## Why a use is not a character either

The use slot fails differently, and it fails in the grammar rather than in the
distribution. Read three uses together.

<Specimen name="猼訑" />

<span lang="zh-Hans">猼訑</span> (bóshī) gives
<span lang="zh-Hant">佩之不畏</span>: wear it and you are not afraid.
<span lang="zh-Hans">鹿蜀</span> gives
<span lang="zh-Hant">佩之宜子孫</span>, wear it and your descendants prosper.
Same verb, different benefit. The other verb is
<span lang="zh-Hant">食之</span>: <span lang="zh-Hans">狌狌</span>'s
<span lang="zh-Hant">食之善走</span>, eat it and you run well. Every one of them
is a sentence whose subject is the reader.

<span lang="zh-Hans">旋龟</span> (xuánguī) shows the same thing from the other
side.

<Specimen name="旋龟" />

It has two records. The <span lang="zh-Hant">南山經之首</span> one ends
<span lang="zh-Hant">佩之不聾，可以為底</span>. The
<span lang="zh-Hant">中次六經</span> one gives the bird's head, the tail and the
sound of splitting wood, and stops. One creature, two records, one use between
them. A property that is present in one record of a creature and absent from
another is a property of the record.

## What you leave with

Two columns you can now justify keeping or dropping, rather than two columns
that were on the card because week 2 put them there.

If you keep the omen column, split it into scope and event so the grid can be
sorted by machine and the pattern is visible to whoever reads your cards. If you
drop it, write one sentence where the column used to be saying why, because a
deleted field and a field nobody thought of look identical afterwards.

Week 6 goes after the last borrowed column: the text's own kind-words, which
look like a rank and are the cheapest thing in the entry to take without
checking.
