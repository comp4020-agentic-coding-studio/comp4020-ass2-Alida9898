## What this week does

Week 2's comparison column, <span lang="zh-Hant">其狀如</span> X, works when X
is one animal you already know. The records worth arguing about put two or three
animals into one body, and one of them puts no animal in at all.

So this week takes a composite body apart into parts and numbers, and tests that
against the three cases most likely to break it:

1. **Two bodies whose inventories differ only in a number** —
   <span lang="zh-Hans">陆吾</span> and <span lang="zh-Hans">开明兽</span>, on
   one mountain, filed apart.
2. **One name whose count moves between its two records** —
   <span lang="zh-Hans">天吴</span>, eight tails in one line and ten in the
   other.
3. **A body assembled from no animal** — <span lang="zh-Hans">帝江</span>,
   shaped like a sack, with no face.

A section after those three collects the eight part-words all four records stay
inside. That vocabulary is the artefact you leave with.

## Example 1: two bodies that differ only in a number

<span lang="zh-Hans">陆吾</span> (lùwú) guards
<span lang="zh-Hant">昆侖</span> (kūnlún), the mountain the text treats as the
god's lower capital.

<Specimen name="陆吾" />

<span lang="zh-Hans">开明兽</span> (kāimíngshòu) stands on the same mountain,
in a different chapter.

<Specimen name="开明兽" />

Take both bodies apart and write down only what the lines supply:

| part | <span lang="zh-Hans">陆吾</span> | <span lang="zh-Hans">开明兽</span> |
|---|---|---|
| body | tiger | tiger |
| face | human | human |
| claw | tiger | — |
| head | — | nine |
| tail | nine | — |

An em dash is the record not saying, which week 2 established is not the same as
a zero.

Two of those five rows are identical. The rest is one number. The whole
difference between these two bodies is that <span lang="zh-Hant">九</span>,
nine, attaches to <span lang="zh-Hant">尾</span> in one line and to
<span lang="zh-Hant">首</span> in the other.

So the part is not the character. The part is the noun the character is stated
in, and the number is the character. That is the half of the rule you can act on
today.

<Caution label="Anatomy is not what the text sorts them by">
  <span lang="zh-Hans">陆吾</span> is a <span lang="zh-Hant">神</span>, and its
  line says what it does: <span lang="zh-Hant">司天之九部及帝之囿時</span>, it
  administers the nine regions of heaven and the seasons of the god's park.
  <span lang="zh-Hans">开明兽</span> carries <span lang="zh-Hant">獸</span>,
  beast, inside its own name.

  Same mountain, nearly the same body, filed apart. A parts inventory cannot see
  the difference, and this week does not fix that. Week 6 is spent on it.
</Caution>

## Example 2: one name whose count moves

<span lang="zh-Hans">天吴</span> (tiānwú) has two records, and they disagree.

<Specimen name="天吴" />

<span lang="zh-Hant">八首人面</span>, eight heads with human faces, is the only
thing both lines say. <span lang="zh-Hant">海外東經</span> gives it eight feet,
eight tails and a blue-yellow back. <span lang="zh-Hant">大荒東經</span> gives
it a tiger's body and ten tails, and mentions no feet.

The previous section said the number is the character. This section says the
number moves. Both lines name <span lang="zh-Hant">天吳</span> and neither is a
copy of the other, so the strongest character in the system is unstable across
two records of one name.

There are three things you can do about that, and only one of them is honest.
You can pick a record and not say which. You can average them, which invents a
creature with nine tails that no line describes. Or you keep both records, each
in its own row, each citing the line it came from. The bestiary does the third.
Its entry for <span lang="zh-Hans">天吴</span> carries two citations and a
`form` field that names both bodies.

## Example 3: a body assembled from no animal

<span lang="zh-Hans">帝江</span> (dìjiāng) is filed on
<span lang="zh-Hant">天山</span>, further along the same western route as
<span lang="zh-Hant">昆侖</span>. Its record breaks the comparison rather than
the count.

<Specimen name="帝江" />

Four clauses, four different problems:

- <span lang="zh-Hant">其狀如黃囊</span>. The comparison slot holds a sack.
  The slot works exactly as designed and points outside the animal kingdom,
  and nothing in the entry form notices.
- <span lang="zh-Hant">六足四翼</span>. Six feet, four wings. Still parts, still
  integers. The vocabulary survives the case it was least likely to survive.
- <span lang="zh-Hant">渾敦無面目</span>. No face. The distinguishing feature is
  an absence.
- <span lang="zh-Hant">是識歌舞</span>. It knows song and dance. Not a part, not
  a count, not a use.

Every other composite in this course is assembled from animals a reader has
already met. This one is assembled from none of them, and its record is no
shorter and no vaguer for it. The finding is about our method, not about the
creature: recognition was never doing the work, so losing it costs less than it
looks.

<Caution label="A stated absence is not a missing value">
  Week 2 gave three reasons a cell can be blank: the material says the value is
  nothing, the material does not say, nobody has checked.
  <span lang="zh-Hant">無面目</span> is the first.
  <span lang="zh-Hans">开明兽</span>'s silence about claws is the second.

  Written carelessly both come out as an em dash, and the one real finding in
  <span lang="zh-Hans">帝江</span>'s body is filed as an oversight.
</Caution>

## The eight words the four records stay inside

All four bodies in one table. Every cell is what a cited line supplies and
nothing else:

<div class="parts-table">

| part | <span lang="zh-Hans">陆吾</span> | <span lang="zh-Hans">开明兽</span> | <span lang="zh-Hans">天吴</span> | <span lang="zh-Hans">帝江</span> |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| body | tiger | tiger | — / tiger | sack |
| head | — | nine | eight | — |
| face | human | human | human | none, stated |
| claw | tiger | — | — | — |
| foot | — | — | eight / — | six |
| wing | — | — | — | four |
| tail | nine | — | eight / ten | — |
| back | — | — | blue-yellow / — | — |
| colour | — | — | — | yellow, red |

</div>

<span lang="zh-Hans">天吴</span> has two records, so where they differ the cell
shows <span lang="zh-Hant">海外東經</span> first and
<span lang="zh-Hant">大荒東經</span> second.
<span lang="zh-Hans">帝江</span>'s body cell holds a sack because that is what
the comparison supplies; it is the only cell in the table that is not an animal
part.

Eight part-words — body, head, face, claw, foot, wing, tail, back — and colour,
which is not a part but attaches to one.
<span lang="zh-Hant">背青黃</span>, a blue-yellow back, uses both at once. Four records, five lines,
and not one of them reaches for a word outside that list. The vocabulary is
closed before the counting starts.

Four bodies side by side is a visual argument and prose cannot hold it. The
[slides](/decks/week-03/) draw the four inventories with the counts as labels.
It is the only deck this semester, and this is the reason.

<Caution label="These eight words are ours">
  They are derived from four records chosen to make this week's argument. The
  <span lang="zh-Hans">山海经</span> (Shānhǎijīng, the *Classic of Mountains and Seas*) has no parts list of its own, and this is
  not a count over the eighteen <span lang="zh-Hant">卷</span>.

  A fifth record can add a ninth word. When one does, that is a result about the
  vocabulary and gets written down as one. It is not a creature that does not
  fit.
</Caution>

## What you leave with

A parts vocabulary, agreed in the room. Write it down before anyone uses it. An
unwritten vocabulary grows a word every time a body does not fit, and nobody
notices the moment it stopped being closed.

This week's specimen card is parts and integers only. Anything that will not
decompose goes under a heading reading *residue*, and the residue is the part of
the card that gets read first.
<span lang="zh-Hans">帝江</span>'s <span lang="zh-Hant">是識歌舞</span> is
residue. So is <span lang="zh-Hans">陆吾</span>'s
<span lang="zh-Hant">司天之九部</span>.

Two of this week's four are filed on a route you can walk:
<span lang="zh-Hans">陆吾</span> and <span lang="zh-Hans">帝江</span> are both
in <span lang="zh-Hant">西山經</span>, a few mountains apart. The other two are
in <span lang="zh-Hant">海內西經</span> and the eastern
<span lang="zh-Hant">海經</span>, which give no route at all. Next week is about
what happens when one habitat column is asked to hold both.
